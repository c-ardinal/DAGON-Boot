Try the [DAGON Console demo](~/demo/) in the browser.

**A beautiful design with a muddy operation.**

A product can give each of its microcontrollers the role that suits it best and have them work together well. Even so, changing a program can mean taking the product apart and swapping cables, or struggling with a different write tool for each microcontroller.

DAGON makes the distributed microcontroller designs that professionals and specialists use workable for a wide range of people, from those starting electronics with AI to individual developers who do not compromise on design.

The first time only, you connect each microcontroller directly, one at a time, and write its factory image. After that, one connection is enough, and you do not need to connect them one at a time.
If that port is outside the case, the case stays closed.
Otherwise the case is opened only for that one connection.
Through one microcontroller connected to your PC, you write to the microcontrollers beyond it with a single operation. More microcontrollers take more time; the operation does not grow.

Update images are all signed and written safely. A microcontroller starts a new version on trial and goes back by itself to the previous version, where a slot holds one, if it does not work.

DAGON handles mixed kinds of microcontroller once you draw the setup.

Applications are written in Rust.

Words such as node and hub are in [What the words mean](/en/explanation/words/).
